# Social roster semantics for generalization scenarios

The generalization catalog in [socialGeneralizationScenarios.ts](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/lib/matchmaking/v3/socialGeneralizationScenarios.ts:1) describes Social Mix rosters and deterministic arrival, pause, and resume boundaries. It does not change the matcher or production policy. The candidate remains opt-in as `courtmate-beneficial-rescue`.

## Structural opportunities and current availability

The beneficial-rescue path builds its own variety context from the full player list and completed-match history, with `includePausedPlayers: true` and no current schedule constraints ([socialBatch.ts](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/lib/matchmaking/v3/socialBatch.ts:478)). That is the opportunity vocabulary used by its courtmate and rolling-type objectives. The legacy context is built separately and may reflect current schedule constraints. Paused participants therefore remain in the candidate's structural denominator even while they are excluded from assignment.

The context builder excludes paused players by default, and retains them when `includePausedPlayers` is enabled ([socialVariety.ts](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/lib/matchmaking/v3/socialVariety.ts:698)). Structural opportunities are generated from the roster's effective sides. In unrestricted Social Mix, MIXED is feasible for every roster member when there are at least two players on each side; OWN_SIDE is feasible for a player only when their own side has at least four players ([socialVariety.ts](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/lib/matchmaking/v3/socialVariety.ts:283)). Busy state and rest do not enter those structural checks. By contrast, the active assignment pool removes both paused and busy participants ([fairness.ts](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/lib/matchmaking/v3/fairness.ts:25)). A busy or resting player can be temporarily unavailable for a refill without losing a structurally feasible type.

That gives the edge rosters distinct per-player denominators:

- At 11/3, upper-side players can structurally play MIXED and OWN_SIDE; lower-side players can play MIXED only.
- At 8/0, every player can play OWN_SIDE only.
- At 3/3, every player can play MIXED only.
- At 10/3, lower-side players can play MIXED only. A lower-side arrival makes the roster 10/4, so OWN_SIDE becomes structurally feasible for all lower-side players, including the new arrival.

These rules determine each player's rolling-type denominator. A one-type player is not scored as missing an impossible type. The catalog tests verify the actual context builder's vocabulary for these cases and verify that a temporary busy flag leaves it unchanged.

## Join, pause, resume, and departure

An event's `afterCompletedMatches` value is the earliest boundary where it should take effect. A benchmark runner should delay a join, pause, or resume if its target is assigned to a match and record the actual completed-match count when the event is applied. The roster helper materializes the nominal catalog state at a completed-match boundary; it does not invent a busy-state or event-deferral policy.

Production joins try to avoid a catch-up burst. The guest route calculates matchmaking credit from non-paused players and stores `arrivalPriorityAt` when an active session receives the guest ([guest credit](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/app/api/sessions/[code]/guests/route.ts:184), [guest creation](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/app/api/sessions/[code]/guests/route.ts:229)). Its credit population includes non-paused players even if they are currently busy. When a paused player returns after other completed matches have occurred, the pause route recalculates the player's credit against the other non-paused players and sets arrival priority for an active session ([resume credit](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/app/api/sessions/[code]/pause-player/route.ts:219), [resume priority](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/app/api/sessions/[code]/pause-player/route.ts:239)). Those queries also include busy players. Both operations use [calculateNoCatchUpMatchmakingCredit](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/lib/matchmaking/matchmakingCredit.ts:19), which credits an entrant up to the minimum effective match count in that population rather than creating a catch-up burst.

The generic simulation helpers in [entry.ts](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/lib/matchmaking/v3/entry.ts:13) and [simulation.ts](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/lib/matchmaking/v3/simulation.ts:102) exclude busy players when calculating their neutral baseline. The roster-generalization runner should use the production credit rule above instead of that helper so its arrival and resume inputs match the API semantics.

Pausing removes a player from assignment but preserves their session-player record and match history. Resume updates availability and queue timing, and may set arrival priority during an active session ([pause-player/route.ts](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/app/api/sessions/[code]/pause-player/route.ts:278)). The candidate's full structural context also retains that paused player. The pause/resume scenarios therefore leave the type and courtmate denominator unchanged while the player is paused.

The production DELETE route refuses removal when the player is busy or has any related match record ([players/[userId]/route.ts](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/app/api/sessions/[code]/players/[userId]/route.ts:221), [history guard](/Users/shazlinsalamat/Documents/Codex/2026-10-05/s/outputs/anti-selek/src/app/api/sessions/[code]/players/[userId]/route.ts:238)). There is no played-participant `LEFT` state in the matcher input. For that reason, the catalog's “played departure” scenario pauses P14 after match 8 and leaves the player paused for the remainder of the run. The candidate continues to consider P14 part of the structural roster; in the 10/4 roster this means OWN_SIDE remains structurally feasible for the paused lower-side player and the other lower-side players. The scenario tests the current production semantics and does not silently redefine departure as deletion.

A real roster change in the catalog is represented by a new lower-side player joining a 10/3 roster after match 8. That changes the structural side count from 10/3 to 10/4. Removing an idle player with no history could also change the roster, but it is not part of this catalog because the requested departure occurs after play has started.

## Side-label symmetry and checkpoints

No mirrored copies of the fixed-ratio cases are included. Exchanging the UPPER and LOWER labels while exchanging the players' sides leaves structural MIXED and OWN_SIDE feasibility unchanged. A seeded matcher property test also mirrors an 8-player 6/2 roster and its completed-history snapshots, then compares selected IDs, partitions, courtmate gain, and rolling-type gain under both production Social and beneficial rescue. This checks a representative realized-layout case without duplicating every catalog run.

For fixed scenarios, the short checkpoint is `round(6 × players / 4)`, which targets roughly six completed appearances per player when four players appear per match. That gives 15 matches for 10 players, 18 for 12, 21 for 14, 24 for 16, and 27 for 18. Dynamic scenarios use the largest roster size during the scenario, so the 12-to-14 arrival case also has a 21-match checkpoint. Scenarios marked `longDiagnostic` are intended for the separate 100-completion diagnostic; the flag itself does not change scoring or scheduling.
